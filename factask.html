Understanding Factorials & Math Guide

The factorial of a non-negative integer n, denoted by n!, is the product of all positive integers less than or equal to n:

n! = n × (n - 1) × (n - 2) × ... × 3 × 2 × 1

Factorials are fundamental in mathematics, including probability, combinatorics, series expansions (such as Taylor and Maclaurin series), and statistical physics.
There are three main mathematical reasons why 0! = 1:
  1. Empty Product Convention: In mathematics, multiplying zero numbers yields the multiplicative identity, which is 1 (just as adding zero numbers yields 0).
  2. Recurrence Relation: Since n! = n × (n - 1)!, solving for (n - 1)! gives (n - 1)! = n! / n. Setting n = 1 yields 0! = 1! / 1 = 1.
  3. Combinatorics: The number of ways to arrange zero items is exactly 1 (the empty set).
Trailing zeros are created by pairs of prime factors 2 and 5 (since 2 × 5 = 10). In any factorial, factors of 2 are far more abundant than factors of 5. Therefore, the number of trailing zeros equals the exponent of the highest power of 5 dividing n!.

By Legendre's Formula:
Zeros = ⌊n/5⌋ + ⌊n/25⌋ + ⌊n/125⌋ + ⌊n/625⌋ + ...

For example, in 100!: ⌊100/5⌋ + ⌊100/25⌋ = 20 + 4 = 24 trailing zeros!
